# Milestone 1: Community and Candidate Distinctions

## Community

**r/AMCsAList** (https://www.reddit.com/r/AMCsAList/): a subreddit for members of AMC Theatres' A-List movie subscription.

- **Public:** yes. It can be read without logging in.
- **Not tied to one show or release:** posts cover many films, theater chains, formats (IMAX, Dolby, ScreenX), concessions, and app issues.
- **Range in quality:** some posts are detailed firsthand reports, and some are one-line reactions or jokes.

I read about 30 posts from the front page (week of October 20, 2026).

## What I noticed while reading

**Flairs aren't reliable labels.** "Untitled Documentary Film Trilogy" is flaired *App Issue*, but it's a joke. "Ramayana" is flaired *Discussion*, but it's a complaint about there being no screenings in LA. I'll need to label posts myself instead of copying the flair.

## Candidate distinctions

### 1. Reviews with specific details vs. reviews with only general praise

Some reviews name things you could check: a theater, a person, a format, a language, an award. Others only give feelings or a grade.

- **Specific:** *"La Bola Negra Is Stunningly Good"* names the Burbank 16, a Q&A with the directors, their Best Director win at Cannes, three timelines, that it's about 95% in Spanish, and a first-time lead actor.
- **General:** *"I went to watch Verity twice"* says the acting and production are "wonderful" and gives it a B+, with no details.
- **In between:** *Street Fighter* says "beautifully shot," "funny moments," and mentions a twist. It names qualities but nothing checkable.

### 2. Questions asking for a fact vs. complaints phrased as questions

Some questions could be answered by another member with a fact. Others are really complaints, and no one in the subreddit could answer them.

- **Asking for a fact:** *"Garden State AMC ScreenX"* asks when the new ScreenX theater opens. *"Is AMC down for anyone else?"* includes the exact error message.
- **Venting as a question:** *"Why are all the studios dropping great stuff in such a short period?"* and *"You can see everything (no showtimes)"* ask how to "push AMC to have more showings."

I think this will be my hardest boundary.

### 3. Firsthand observation vs. opinion or speculation

Some posts report something the writer saw or was told. Others share an opinion or a guess about why something is happening.

- **Firsthand:** *"Has AMC discontinued those cardboard popcorn boxes?"*: a concessions worker at The Grove said the decision "came from the top," and the writer also couldn't get one at Burbank 16. *"The first five minutes of FJORD"* describes the audience going from scoffing to laughing at the studio logos.
- **Opinion or speculation:** *"Why are all the studios dropping…"* predicts that "most will flop at the box office."

### 4. (Possible) Complaints about showtimes and availability

A lot of posts are about not finding a showing or format nearby: *Musk*, *Ramayana*, *You Can See Everything*, the Fort Worth showtimes post, and *Garden State ScreenX*. I'm not sure yet whether this is its own label or overlaps with #2. I'll decide in Milestone 2.

## Posts to leave out of the dataset

- **Mod and megathread posts** (*AMC Screen Unseen Megathread*, *AMC Issues Megathread*, *What Format Should I See This In?!*): rules and FAQs, not community posts.
- **Link or image-only posts** (the *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* IMAX poster, *The Prestige* re-release): almost no text. Their comments might still be usable.
